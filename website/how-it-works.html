How Yellowback works

A vault, a price, and a rule the miners enforce. That's the whole machine. This page walks through each part with pictures, then the numbers.

Anatomy of a vault: two doors, one key.

A Yellowback vault is an ordinary Ycash pay-to-script-hash output with a timelock (CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY) — a kind of script every Ycash node has enforced for years. It has exactly two ways out.

Nobody opens it early

Before the unlock height, not even your own key can spend the vault. The network rejects it outright.

A healthy vault is yours forever

Door 2 only opens for a vault worth less than 110% of its debt. Stay above that and there is no deadline to redeem.

Even a claim gives back the rest

A claimant keeps at most the debt's value plus a 10% margin, priced at the claim price. Anything above that is paid back to your address in the same transaction.

netstat --yellowback

Who does what: the network map.

Four lines, no central station. Each party only does its own job, and none of them can touch your vault.

mint line

You

Lock YEC, mint YED, spend it, and later redeem. Your key is the only one on your vault.

price line

Attestors

Anyone can post a timelocked bond and sign YEC/USD prices off-chain. Mints and claims carry a handful of these signatures.

enforcement

Mining pools

Put a price quote in every block they mine, keep rule-breaking Yellowback transactions out, and refuse blocks that release collateral without a burn.

wallet line

Wallets

YecWallet (desktop, full node) has a Yellowback tab; YEW, the mobile wallet, reaches the chain through lightwalletd.

yed_getprice

Where the price comes from: two sources, cautious math.

A dollar on a blockchain is only as good as its price feed. Yellowback uses two independent populations — hashpower and bonded money — and always picks the answer that protects the system.

Slow on purpose

The pool side of the mint price is the lowest of three rolling medians, so a sudden spike can't be used to over-mint. A crash shows up within about two hours.

No price, no mint

If too few recent blocks carry a quote, the price is undefined and minting simply stops. Redemptions never need a price.

Accountable in public

Every quote sits in a permanent block next to the pool's payout key. Attestors sign with keys tied to an on-chain bond.

Pick a lock term. Longer is cheaper.

Three term classes. The share of your collateral you can mint (in a calm market) grows with the lock length.

class A

30 – 90 days

mint up to 20%

500% backing. For a short bridge — a bill, a purchase, a gap until payday.

class B

90 days – 1 year

mint up to 25%

400% backing. The everyday choice: a season or a year of dollars.

class C

1 – 5 years

mint up to 33%

300% backing. For long-term holders who are happy to let the coins sleep.

The volatility multiplier. When YEC's recent price swings are wilder than the reference level, every class's backing requirement is multiplied — smoothly — by up to 3×. Calm market, 1×. It only affects new mints; it never changes a vault you already opened.
./simulate --yec 10000 --price 0.40 --term 1y

What if YEC moves? Three stories.

Same start every time: 10,000 YEC at $0.40 ($4,000) locked for a year at 400%, minting $1,000 of YED. Your debt is in dollars, so it never grows.

YEC +150%

The moon

vault value $10,000

Return $1,000 of YED, unlock 10,000 YEC worth $10,000. You had dollars all year and kept every coin of the rally.

YEC −50%

The dip

vault value $2,000 · 200% of debt

Still healthy. Return $1,000 of YED whenever you're ready — there's no deadline above 110% — and wait out the recovery with all 10,000 YEC.

YEC −80%

The crash

vault value $800 · 80% of debt

Underwater. You can still redeem, or walk away: you keep the $1,000 you minted and, 30 days after unlock, a YED holder can claim the vault by burning $1,000 of YED. The worst case is that the collateral pays the debt.

Safety rails that fail closed.

When something looks wrong, Yellowback stops creating dollars. It never stops minters from getting their collateral back.

RailTriggerWhat happens
No priceToo few recent blocks carry a quoteNew mints pause. Redemptions continue.
DivergenceThe price sources disagree by more than 20%New mints pause until they converge.
Global ratioAll collateral together falls below 250% of all YEDOnly mints that themselves raise the ratio (backed ≥ 500%) are accepted.
Supply capYED supply reaches 15% of YEC's market capOnly heavily backed mints (≥ 500%) go through.
ParticipationFewer than 60% of recent blocks come from enforcing poolsNew mints pause; block enforcement continues down to 50%.
SunsetA release reaches its enforcement end height (~1 year)Old releases stop enforcing so they never split from upgraded ones.

Figures are the first mainnet parameter set. The authoritative list is in the documentation.
