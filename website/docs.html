Overview

Ycash Yellowback (YED) is a decentralized US-dollar token on the Ycash blockchain. You create YED by locking YEC in a timelocked vault and minting up to a fixed fraction of the vault's value; you get the YEC back by returning (burning) the same amount of YED after the lock ends. 1 YED = $1, counted in whole cents.

Yellowback is an overlay on ordinary Ycash transactions: vaults are standard pay-to-script-hash outputs with CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY, YED live on small transparent outputs described by one OP_RETURN note per transaction, and every Yellowback-aware node computes the same state from the same chain. The one rule a Ycash script cannot express — collateral may only leave a vault if its debt is burned — is enforced by mining pools that run the Yellowback node.

In one sentence: lock YEC, mint dollars, spend them, and later return the dollars to unlock every coin — you never have to sell your Ycash.

Quick start

Yellowback is used through YecWallet, the desktop full-node Ycash wallet, which has a Yellowback tab (mint, send, redeem, claim, sweep) and bundles a Yellowback-enabled ycashd. A mobile wallet, YEW, is in development.

  1. Install and sync a Yellowback-enabled YecWallet. The node feature is behind the -yellowback flag; the wallet sets it for you.
  2. Fund the wallet with YEC: the collateral, plus the enforcement fee for both the mint and the later redemption, plus ordinary network fees.
  3. Estimate: choose an amount in dollars and a lock term; the wallet shows the YEC required at the current mint price.
  4. Mint: confirm. Your YED appear on a ye… address once the transaction is mined.
  5. Use your YED: send, receive, hold.
  6. Redeem after the unlock height: the wallet burns the YED, pays the fee, and returns your collateral in one step.
ycash-cli · example
# how much YEC to mint $1,000 for ~1 year (420,480 blocks)?
$ ycash-cli yed_estimatecollateral 100000 420480
# → requiredZat, termClass, lockHeight, claimHeight, minRatioBps, pMint …

$ ycash-cli yed_mint 100000 420480
$ ycash-cli yed_getbalance
$ ycash-cli yed_send ye1…recipient 2500       # $25.00

# a year later, at or after lockHeight:
$ ycash-cli yed_redeem <vaultTxid>

Amounts in RPCs are integer US cents; lock lengths are in blocks (75-second blocks: 1,152 per day, 420,480 per year). Exact argument lists are in the RPC reference and the node's doc/yellowback-rpc.md.

Try it without real money. The project ships a regtest devnet with simulated pools, attestors and users, so you can mint, redeem and even watch a liquidation on one laptop. See contrib/yellowback/devnet/ in the node repository.

Lifecycle of a position

Mint

One transaction locks the collateral, creates the YED, pays the enforcement fee and records a note describing which output carries how many cents. It commits to a recent reference height, and the collateral it needs is computed from the price snapshot at that height. A mint must lock at least $100 and at most $10,000 of YED; open several vaults for more.

Hold & transfer

YED move like coins: a transfer spends YED outputs and creates new ones; the totals must balance. YED addresses start with ye on mainnet.

Redeem

From the unlock height on, the owner can spend the vault by burning exactly the vault's debt in YED (any YED) and paying the enforcement fee. Redemption never depends on a price and is never paused by a halt. A healthy vault has no deadline.

Void mints

Rarely, a mint can lose a race with the supply cap or be invalidated by a deep chain reorganisation. It is then VOID: no YED were created, and the collateral stays locked until the unlock height, after which the wallet's Redeem button releases it with no burn and no fee.

Because every redemption pays the enforcement fee, a mint must lock at least four times the minimum fee. If no pool quoted recently, no enforcement fee is required, so a redemption never waits on miners.

Claims & liquidation

There is no liquidation engine, no keeper and no auction. A vault can only change hands by the claim path, and only when all of these hold:

  • the chain is at or past the vault's claim height (unlock height + 30 days);
  • the vault is underwater: worth less than 110% of its debt at the claim price (or, under the emergency rule for extreme crashes, below a lower threshold shown twice at least an hour apart);
  • the claimant burns the full debt in YED.

The claimant receives collateral worth at most the debt plus a 10% margin at the claim price (no margin under the emergency rule); everything above that is returned to the owner in the same transaction. Owners see a warning in the wallet when someone posts an emergency notice against their vault.

Think of it as non-recourse. If YEC crashes and you choose not to redeem, the worst outcome is that your collateral pays your debt. You keep the dollars you minted, and nobody can come after anything else you own.

Price feed

Pools. Each enforcing pool writes a YEC/USD quote (from exchange sources it chooses) into the coinbase of every block it mines. The node keeps rolling medians over the last 96, 576 and 2,016 blocks; a window needs at least half (fast) or two-thirds (mid, slow) of its blocks to carry a quote before it yields a price.

Attestors. Anyone can register as an attestor by posting a timelocked YEC bond. Attestors sign prices off-chain and gossip them; a mint or claim carries four to six of those signatures in a small extra input, and the nodes combine them by a bond-weighted quantile.

Combination. The mint price is the lower of the pool price and the attestor price; the claim price is the higher. An attacker who wants to over-mint or to liquidate healthy vaults must move both populations at once.

Enforcement & activation

Pools that run the Yellowback node do three things: publish quotes, keep rule-breaking Yellowback transactions out of their block templates, and — once activated — refuse to build on a block that releases vault collateral without burning its debt. To a stock Ycash node, every block an enforcing pool mines is valid; nothing a stock node accepts today is ever rejected by it. This is a soft fork carried by mining software: no new opcode, no branch ID, no network upgrade.

  • Activation: minting is impossible until 75% of blocks in a 2,016-block window signal support, plus a further 2,016-block delay — no YED can exist before the network is ready to protect it.
  • Work valve: if a chain the node rejected gets six blocks of work ahead, the node stops enforcing for the session and follows the majority chain, so enforcers can't split off.
  • Sunset: each release stops rejecting blocks at its enforcement end height, so a stale release never splits from an upgraded one.

Privacy

Ycash is a privacy coin: YEC can be held and sent in shielded (Sapling) addresses. YED, however, lives on transparent outputs, and vault collateral is transparent while it is locked — the miners who enforce the rules have to be able to read them. Your other YEC can stay shielded the whole time. Shielded YED is an open research item.

Risks

Read this before minting. Yellowback is new, experimental software. Nothing on this site is financial advice.
  • Price crashes. If YEC falls far enough that your vault is below 110% of its debt after the grace period, it can be claimed. You keep the YED you minted; you lose collateral up to the debt plus 10%.
  • Miner participation. The burn rule is enforced by pools. If enforcing pools fall below half of recent blocks, block enforcement pauses and abandoned vaults are protected only by pools that still filter. A sustained hashpower majority that stops enforcing could release collateral without a burn — the same assumption the chain already makes about reorganisations.
  • Price-feed manipulation. Moving the price requires a sustained majority of quote-tagged blocks and corrupting bonded attestors.
  • Software bugs. A bug in the enforcement code could make enforcing pools orphan blocks; the work valve and kill switch bound this, but don't eliminate it.
  • Pools see claims first. A pool can front-run a claim in a block it mines.
  • Locked means locked. You cannot get your collateral back before the unlock height, for any reason.
  • The dollar peg is not guaranteed. YED is redeemable against vaults, not against a bank account; its market price depends on demand and on the backing.

Project status

As of October 2026: the overlay is implemented and tested on both Ycash node lines and in YecWallet; a security audit has been completed and remediated; the 6.21.0 release candidate is being prepared; and the first mainnet parameter set (start height 3,075,000) is set. Minting on mainnet requires pools to signal support and pass a published pool-diversity bar (at least three independent pools, none above 40% of quote-tagged blocks).

Yellowback's design draws on DigiByte's DigiDollar; it diverges where Ycash's architecture differs, most visibly in replacing consensus opcodes with miner enforcement.
