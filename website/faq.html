Frequently asked questions

Short, honest answers. For the full picture, see How it works and the documentation.

The basics

What is Yellowback, in one sentence?
A decentralized dollar on Ycash: you lock YEC in a vault only you can open, mint YED against it (1 YED = $1), and when the lock ends you return the YED to get every YEC back.
Why is it called “Yellowback”?
From 1863 to 1933 the US issued gold certificates — paper dollars redeemable for gold, with backs printed in gold-orange ink. People called them “yellowbacks.” Yellowback revives the idea of money you can redeem for a hard asset, with Ycash as the gold and you holding the vault key.
Yellowback or YED — which is which?
Yellowback is the system; YED is the unit. Like Bitcoin and BTC: “Yellowback is a dollar on Ycash”, “I minted 250 YED.”
Do I ever have to sell my Ycash?
No. That's the point. Instead of selling YEC to get dollars, you borrow dollars against it from yourself. At the end of the lock you return the dollars and get all your YEC back — including any price gains.
Who issues YED? Is there a company behind it?
Nobody issues it but you. Every YED is created by someone locking their own YEC in their own vault. There is no company, bank, foundation or federation holding reserves or keys.

Minting & unlocking

How much can I mint?
Up to 1/5 of the vault's value for a 30–90 day lock, 1/4 for 90 days to a year, and 1/3 for 1–5 years, in a calm market. When YEC is volatile the requirement rises by up to 3×. Each vault mints between $100 and $10,000. Try the calculator.
Can I unlock early?
No. The timelock is enforced by every Ycash node; not even your own key can spend the vault before the unlock height. Choose a term you're comfortable with.
What do I need to give back?
Exactly the number of YED you minted — any YED, they're interchangeable — plus the enforcement fee in YEC. If you spent your YED, you can acquire YED from someone else to redeem.
Is there a deadline to redeem after the unlock height?
Not while your vault is healthy (worth at least 110% of its debt at the claim price). Door 2, the claim path, only opens for underwater vaults, and only 30 days after the unlock height.
Does my debt grow over time? Is there interest?
No interest. Your debt is a fixed number of dollars. The costs are the enforcement fee at mint and at redemption (the greater of 0.5 YEC or 0.25% of the collateral), a small attestation fee, and ordinary network fees.
What happens if YEC crashes?
If your vault stays above 110% of its debt, nothing — just redeem when you're ready. If it falls below and you leave it there past the 30-day grace period, a YED holder can claim it by burning your full debt. They receive collateral worth at most the debt plus 10%; any remainder returns to you. You keep the dollars you minted either way.

Trust & safety

Can anyone freeze, seize or block my redemption?
No one holds a key to your vault but you, and no committee sits between you and your collateral. Redemptions don't depend on a price and are never paused by the safety rails.
What stops someone from unlocking collateral without paying back the YED?
A Ycash script can't check that by itself, so mining pools running the Yellowback node enforce it: they keep such transactions out of their blocks and, once activated, refuse to build on any block that contains one. The same majority-of-hashpower assumption already secures the chain against reorganisations.
Where does the price come from?
Two independent sources: quotes that mining pools put in every block (rolling medians over ~2, ~12 and ~42 hours) and signatures from bonded attestors. Minting uses the lower of the two; claims use the higher.
Does Yellowback change Ycash's consensus rules?
Not for anyone who doesn't opt in. There's no new opcode, branch ID or network upgrade. A stock Ycash node accepts every block an enforcing pool mines. Enforcing pools add one extra block-validity check, which makes Yellowback a soft fork carried by mining software.
Is it audited?
An ecosystem-wide security audit was completed in October 2026 and its findings remediated on both node lines. Audits reduce risk; they don't remove it. Yellowback is new software — use amounts you can afford to lock.

Privacy & the cypherpunk bit

Is YED private like shielded YEC?
Not yet. YED lives on transparent outputs and vault collateral is transparent while locked, because the pools that enforce the rules must be able to read them. Your other YEC can stay in shielded addresses. Shielded YED is an open research question.
Why does a privacy coin need a dollar?
Because people need stable money without asking permission. Ycash comes from the cypherpunk tradition — Bitcoin's peer-to-peer cash and Zcash's zero-knowledge proofs — and Yellowback adds a dollar that no bank issues and no one can freeze.
Is the code open?
Yes. The node, wallets, pool software and tools are open source. Every Yellowback-aware node computes the same state from the same chain, so you can verify balances, vaults and prices yourself.

Getting started

Is Yellowback live?
It's in release-candidate testing. The first mainnet parameter set starts at block 3,075,000. Minting opens only after 75% of blocks over a ~2-week window signal support, plus a further ~2-week delay.
What wallet do I use?
YecWallet, the desktop full-node Ycash wallet, with its Yellowback tab. YEW, a mobile wallet, is in development.
I run a mining pool. What do I do?
Run the Yellowback-enabled node, set a payout address, and run the quote agent against exchange price sources of your choice. Hashers' GPU software doesn't change. In return your pool earns enforcement fees in proportion to the blocks it quotes. See the enforcement docs.
Can I become a price attestor?
Yes — anyone can, by posting a timelocked YEC bond and running the attestor agent. No domain, open port or hot wallet needed. Attestors earn attestation fees.
