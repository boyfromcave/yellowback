## 1. Finding

A YED claim (selector 4 on the vault's APP branch) moves the whole vault into intents. When RED-5 says a residual is due, the claim must carry exactly two intents: the claimant's, and the owner's residual paying `PQPKH(owner)`. Three rules together let an owner who holds (or rents, or bribes) **one** attestor seat take collateral out of a vault that should have been liquidated, while the debt is re-issued (CAN-1 refunds the burn) and YED holders carry the shortfall:

- **NEW-1 (role by first match, no claimant floor).** `RedVerdict` labels as RESIDUAL the *first intent in vout order* that pays the owner's hash with `nValue ≥ residualZat`; the other becomes CLAIMANT, with no lower bound on its value. An owner self-claiming by path (b) can put the big intent first (labelled residual, uncancellable) and a small one second (labelled claimant) — degenerately a residual of the **entire collateral** and a 1-zat claimant funded from the owner's own coins (the primitive's S-2 only needs `Σ I ≥ V`). The seat cancels the claimant intent: I-2 needs a re-lock ≥ *that intent's* value only, so the vault is re-created with 61,905 YEC — or 1 zat — and the full debt, and the burn is refunded. **Up to 100 % of the collateral extracted.**
- **W3-Y-1 (residual releases unconditionally).** Even with honest labels, the residual intent cannot be cancelled (`intent-cancel-residual`), cannot be re-locked alongside the claimant intent (S-1: one template input per transaction) and its release is unconditional for the module. Cancel the claimant intent, release the residual after 576 blocks.
- **W3-Y-5 (sizing at the wrong price).** Path (b) decides "underwater" at `pEmerg = min(xClaim, aClaim)` against 105 % but sizes the claimant cap at `pClaim = max(...)` with no margin. Corrected magnitudes (verifier, recomputed here with the module's integer formulas): path (b) is **reachable only when `pClaim/pEmerg > 1.25/1.05 = 1.1905`** (below that every vault under 105 % at `pEmerg` is under 125 % at `pClaim` and takes path (a), whose residual is exactly 0); when reachable the residual is **≥ 20 % of the collateral** (exactly 20 % at the path-(a) boundary, 26.7 % at a 30 % spread, 36.5 % at 50 %, 52 % at 100 %). Worked example: D = $100,000, 300,000 YEC, `pClaim` 0.42, `pEmerg` 0.349 → claimant 238,095.24 YEC, residual 61,904.76 YEC (20.63 %); after the cancel the vault holds 238,095 YEC against $100,000: 100 % at `pClaim`, 83.1 % at `pEmerg`, a **$16,905 backing shortfall** at `pEmerg`. The first wave's "5–20 %" and the design doc's "roughly 5 % of D" (R-1c) are wrong; so is the upgrade plan's "≈ 5 % of the debt" in U-24 "given up (iv)".

Preconditions: one seat (10,000–20,000 YEC bond, returned 14 days after exit, never slashed for this), a ≥ 19.05 % attestor/pool disagreement persisting `EMERGENCY_PERSIST` blocks for a standing notice — the regime path (b) exists for. The owner need not self-claim for W3-Y-1: any third party's path-(b) claim pays the owner the residual, and the owner's seat then cancels the claimant intent. The re-created vault is not profitably claimable (a claimant would burn D for ≤ D of collateral), so it stays open as a husk; and the same seat protects it (W3-Y-2). The golden vector replays the W3-Y-1 sequence (claim 300 → cancel 301 → residual release at claim + 11) and pins it as **valid** (`test_yellowback_model.py:1762-1781`). Byte-identical on both lines.

**"Cancel the residual together with the claimant intent"** (the remedial-priority alternative) is not implementable without a primitive change: S-1 (`vault/state.cpp:478`) admits one template input per transaction, and a residual cancelled on its own into a second V output would be a second position (the module addresses positions by one `txid:0` outpoint, `state.cpp:850-854`). Rejected for this tier; CAN-3's hold reaches the same end state (the residual stays tied to the position) inside the module.

**The sizing choice (W3-Y-5) needs an owner ruling — it reverses v3 R1.** R1 (`yellowback-v3-development-plan.md:634-637`, `:1580`) sized path (b) at `pClaim = max` *on purpose*: in the "attestor set colludes low" row (`:1500`) a captured `aClaim` drags `pEmerg` down and fires path (b) on a healthy vault; at `pClaim` sizing the claimant gets exactly D worth at the honest pool price, so "a captured attestor set has nothing to collect". At `pEmerg` sizing the claimant gets `D/pEmerg`, worth `D·pClaim/pEmerg` at the honest price: a colluding-low claim that escapes the cancel brake **earns `(spread − 1)·D`, at least 19 % of D** and up to the whole collateral (a 200 % vault under a 2× spread hands the claimant 100 % of it). The reverse holds in the "attestors collude high" row, which is what path (b) was built for (`:1499`): there `pEmerg = xClaim` is the honest price and R1's sizing pays the honest claimant `D·pEmerg/pClaim ≤ 0.84·D` of value for burning D — **so path (b) never fires in its own design case** (NEW-2). The two rows cannot both be served: path (b) exists only above a 1.19× disagreement, and no single cap pays ≥ D at the lower price while paying ≤ D at the higher one. Recommendation:

- **A1 (recommended): size at `pEmerg`.** It is the only sizing under which path (b) liquidates anything (NEW-2), it bounds the residual below `0.05·D/pEmerg` (4.70 % of D in the example: 13,467 YEC) and it leaves the re-created vault at ≥ 100 % at `pEmerg` and ≥ 119 % at `pClaim` — **claimable by path (a) with a profit**, so a husk does not persist. The colludes-low row is then defended by the cancel brake alone — which is exactly what the brake is for (design doc "Requirement B"), and which W3-Y-2 (CAN-2) makes bounded and non-exhaustible. Record the revision of R1 as a decision.
- **A2 (fallback, if the owner keeps R1): keep `pClaim` sizing.** The floor and CAN-3 still close the extraction completely (R is held; the owner never gains by the cancel), but NEW-2 stands: path (b) is a self-liquidation path only and does not fire in the colludes-high row. Document that in U-24 and IT-3; the rest of this report is unchanged except step 2.

Either way the golden regenerates once (steps 1 and 3 change it); decide A1/A2 **before** the first commit.

**Calibration input (wave 4, W4-K-3).** yb-calibration justified `claimThresholdBps` 12,500, `emergencyRatioBps` 10,500 (KEEP) and the 10,000-YEC bond floor without any cancel or residual mechanics. Raising θ from 11,000 to 12,500 moved the first reachable path-(b) spread from 4.76 % to 19.05 % and the minimum path-(b) residual under today's `pClaim` sizing from 9.09 % to 20.00 % of the collateral (`1 − 1/θ`): rarer, but each W3-Y-1 extraction at least 2.2× larger. Under A1 the residual no longer depends on θ (it is `< 0.05·D/pEmerg`); under A2 it stays `≥ (1 − 1/θ)·C`, so A2 also needs θ re-justified. Give the owner both figures with the A1/A2 question. These are the audit's figures (W4-K-3 and its verification); the ruling does not wait for yb-calibration's re-runs, which come after this report lands (`MEDIUM-W4-K-3-…` §4 step 8) and may prompt a later revisit — so there is no dependency cycle.

**Rule-4 four-part check (PR body).** DigiByte does **X** = liquidate an under-collateralised DigiDollar position in one transaction that pays the liquidator and returns the surplus, in **Y** = `ref/digibyte/src/digidollar/` (validation-time rules), using **M** = finality at inclusion (no challenge window, no veto, so no "half-cancelled" liquidation exists). The Ycash equivalent **Z** = the vault primitive's APP branch into intents plus the attestor set's I-2 cancel (`vault/state.cpp:544-551`, upgrade plan §3.4), which can undo one intent of a two-intent claim and lacks **M**. So the adaptation **W** = module rules that (i) make the cancellable intent carry at least the claimant's share (RED-5 floor, roles by position), (ii) keep the non-cancellable residual tied to the position it came from until that position closes (CAN-3), and (iii) size path (b) at the price that judged the vault underwater (W3-Y-5, A1).
**In-tier ladder.** Tier 3 is decided (R3/O-1). A **module** rule (not the primitive: S-1/I-2 stay application-agnostic, the bridge's intents are unaffected), on the **existing** template branches (no opcode, no template, no transaction field), with **per-vault** state (one appended field on `VaultRecord`, one on `IntentRecord`; no global table). No cheaper rung works: a wallet or relay rule cannot stop an owner-built claim or a seat's cancel, and the primitive's I-2 cannot know which intent was the owner's.

## 7. Risks and side effects

- **Chain split:** none on public networks (no activation height). Both lines must land together; a line without RED-5a accepts the NEW-1 claim the other rejects. Regtest/devnet chains are wiped (`SCHEMA_VERSION` rebuild).
- **Golden freeze:** regenerate before P8 freezes the golden; coordinate with HIGH-W3-Y-2 so the golden and `SCHEMA_VERSION` move **once** if both are ready (preferred), else twice in order.
- **Economics (A1):** a colluding-low path-(b) claim that escapes the cancel brake now pays the colluders `(spread − 1)·D`; the brake is the only defence of that row. This is why A1 should ship with CAN-2 (non-exhaustible brake) and why the owner must sign the R1 revision. yb-calibration has no model of this today (W4-K-3), and its simulator still runs on the retired v3 parameter set (W4-K-1). Before the release it must re-pin to the upgrade line, model claim intents, the one-member cancel, CAN-1 refunds, residual release and CAN-3's hold, and re-run T5 with the chosen sizing. Until then θ, `emergencyRatioBps` and the bond floor are marked "not calibrated against W3-Y-1/W3-Y-2" in its reports.
- **Related owner decision (W4-K-2):** the claim of a re-created or large vault is liquidatable only up to the executable-claim size at measured book depth (about $34,675 in yb-calibration's fees study), and MINT-6b's per-vault cap no longer bounds vaults to that size on the circulating-supply base. A1's "a husk does not persist" argument assumes a claimant can execute the path-(a) claim; for vaults above that size it rests on the per-vault cap being re-decided. Cross-reference: `MEDIUM-W4-K-2-ycash-mint6b-per-vault-cap-owner-decision.md` (the per-vault cap owner decision; land in the same release).
- **Honest owners:** a wrongly claimed and cancelled owner waits for its residual until it closes the position; it can redeem at any time (it must burn the debt, as any redeem).
- **External claim builders** that put the residual first become invalid (`red5-residual`); `yed_claim` and YEW already put the claimant intent at vout 0 (`txbuilder.cpp:178`, YEW `core/src/build/claim.rs:484-524`).
- **Clients:** YEW recomputes and cross-checks `residualZat` at `pClaim` (`core/src/params.rs:613-628`, `core/src/build/terms.rs:1071-1092`) and would refuse every A1 path-(b) row as `inconsistent` — update it to size at `pEmerg` on path (b) (the lightwalletd/yindex vault row must then carry `pEmerg`; check the row schema) and show "held" residuals. YecWallet's claim result text "anyone may release it to the owner after the delay" (`yellowbacktab.cpp:1230`) and the owner's residual note (`:1299-1300`) must say "held until the vault closes if the claim is cancelled"; its `releaseIntent` (`yellowbackcontroller.cpp:1172-1176`, `vault_release`) surfaces the new refusal. chain-viz: optional held marker.
